▶ Chairman's Verdict
Where the Council Agrees
Run Supabase migration first Hide TODO shell pages End-to-end demo path must work Auth is post-signature

Run the Supabase migration first. Every advisor named it a blocker. Hide or kill the three TODO shell pages before any human sees the product — blank pages destroy credibility faster than missing features. The end-to-end demo path (claim in → decision out → result displays) must work reliably before anything else is touched.

Where the Council Clashes
Ship fast vs. audit trail first Minimum viable trust vs. MVP features

The core split: minimum viable trust vs. minimum viable product. The Executor and First Principles Thinker say ship the working flow and defer everything else. The Contrarian says the audit trail is the product — without immutable, traceable decisions, the legal admissibility claim is fiction and sophisticated insurance buyers will catch it. The Expansionist wants to accelerate into CERC growth plays before the foundation exists, which all five reviewers correctly identified as premature.

The chairman sides with the Contrarian on one specific point: for an energy/insurance enterprise buyer, the audit trail is not a nice-to-have. It is what they are actually buying. A fast, pretty, unaudited black box is worthless to a claims adjuster who will testify about it.

Blind Spots the Council Caught
Gap 1 — Legal exposure at the moment of demo
If the pilot customer asks "who is liable when this decision is wrong?" and your UI and PDF contain no "advisory output only" disclaimer, you may create an implied legal obligation before a contract exists. Every reviewer flagged this. Fix it before a human sees a demo output.
Gap 2 — Demo data provenance
No one verified: does the local DuckDB sample actually contain NOAA station records for the buyer's geography? If the demo claim hits a missing partition, F1=1.000 is irrelevant. Validate the exact demo query against actual sample data before any deployment step.
Gap 3 — Supabase free tier single point of failure
Free tier has row limits, cold-start latency, and no SLA. A Supabase outage during the demo kills the deal. Have a local DuckDB fallback or know exactly what fails and how to recover in under 60 seconds.
The Recommendation — Priority Order
  • 1Add liability disclaimer. "Advisory output only — not a legal determination" in every claim output surface and PDF footer. Do this before anything is shown to a buyer.
  • 2Run the Supabase migration with realistic seed data (15–20 real wind farm claims).
  • 3Hide the three TODO pages from navigation entirely — do not delete, just suppress from the sidebar.
  • 4Validate the demo query against local DuckDB sample data. Confirm the exact NOAA partition exists for the buyer's geography before deploying.
  • 5Deploy to Vercel. Wire Vercel's built-in function logs (not Sentry — too much setup for current stage).
  • 6Build the one-page PDF output: NOAA Rule 803(8) citation, station ID, observation timestamp, decision, claim ID. This is the highest-leverage deliverable in the entire stack — converts a demo into a document a legal team can review.
  • 7One hardcoded demo account. No auth system — one API key, one set of credentials that works.
  • 8Write the 60-second demo script before the demo, not after.

Defer entirely: full auth system, Sentry, onboarding flow, analytics page, CERC expansion plays.

▶ The One Thing to Do First
Add the liability disclaimer. Not the migration, not the deploy — the disclaimer. Every other step builds toward showing this product to a buyer. Without that language ("Advisory output only — not a legal determination"), the first successful demo is also a legal risk event. Two lines of text in the UI and PDF footer protect you from implied warranty claims before a contract is signed. It costs 10 minutes. The absence of it is the only thing that can terminate the deal retroactively.

Peer Review Highlights

All 5 reviewers independently chose Response C (First Principles Thinker) as strongest. The convergence was unusual — 5/5 with no dissent. The reason: it was the only response that thought from the buyer's evaluation criteria rather than from the builder's feature list.

Response D (Expansionist) was flagged as the most dangerous — not wrong in direction, but dangerously premature. Growth strategy before live data exists is scope creep dressed as ambition.

The shared blind spot across all advisors: contractual liability at the moment of demo. None of the five considered what happens when a buyer's legal team asks who owns the adjudication output and whether the platform indemnifies against wrong decisions. This is the actual gate for enterprise B2B — not auth, not onboarding, not feature completeness.
